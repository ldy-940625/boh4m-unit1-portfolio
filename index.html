Foundations of management

Good management turns a plan into daily results.

This portfolio explains how an organization works, how managers judge performance, and how they choose an approach that fits the situation.

Explore the basics
Running example
One café is doing well. Should it open a second location?

The owner needs reliable information, clear roles, and a plan for people as well as costs. The café example carries through the site to show how each decision could work in practice.

01 / The organization

People working toward a shared purpose

An organization is a group of people who coordinate their work and resources to reach goals. A café, hospital, charity, or school all fit this definition.

A manager helps that group choose goals, assign work, support people, and check whether the work is achieving the intended result.

An organization as an open system

It exchanges resources and information with the world around it. The café depends on suppliers and customers, then learns from what they say and do.

Inputs

From the environment

Staff, ingredients, money, customer needs, and market information.

→
Transformation

Work inside the café

Plan shifts, prepare food, serve customers, and solve service problems.

→
Outputs

Back to the environment

Meals, service, wages, waste, and customer experiences.

Feedback loop: Reviews, sales, and staff suggestions return to managers and shape the next plan.
02 / Organizational performance

Three ways to ask if the work is going well

These measures answer different questions. A café could serve more orders and still disappoint customers or use too much labour.

01

Productivity

How much output the café produces compared with the resources it uses.

Example: orders served per staff hour
02

Effectiveness

Whether the café reaches the goal it set, such as dependable service and satisfied customers.

Question: did we achieve the goal?
03

Efficiency

How carefully it uses time, money, ingredients, and labour to reach that goal.

Question: what resources did it take?

How the difference matters

If a rushed team serves 20% more orders but makes more mistakes, productivity rises while effectiveness may fall. A manager should check customer feedback as well as order counts before calling the change a success.

03 / Today’s workplace

What managers now have to watch

Globalization

Suppliers and competitors may be far away. The café can buy from more places, but shipping costs or delays can affect its menu.

Diversity

Staff and customers bring different backgrounds and needs. Fair scheduling, respectful communication, and accessible service make the team stronger.

Technology

Online orders and sales reports can reveal busy times. Managers still need to check data quality and protect customer information.

04 / Management levels

Different decisions at different levels

As an organization grows, managers divide responsibility. In a small café, one person may cover more than one level.

Top managersSet direction and major goals
Middle managersTurn goals into plans across teams or locations
First-line managersGuide daily work and support frontline staff

For the café: owner → area manager → shift supervisor.

The manager’s job

Four functions that work together

Opening a second location is more than choosing an address. Each function answers a practical question.

PlanningWhat goal and budget make sense?
OrganizingWho does each task, and what resources are needed?
LeadingHow will the team understand and support the plan?
ControllingWhat results will we track and adjust?
