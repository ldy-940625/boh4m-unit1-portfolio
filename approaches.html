Three ways to think about management

Classical ideas improve structure and efficiency. Behavioural ideas focus on people. Modern approaches ask how the whole organization fits its changing environment. A useful manager can draw from all three.

Infographic 01 / Classical

Make work clear and consistent

Classical management grew from the need to coordinate large amounts of work. It looks closely at tasks, roles, and authority.

The central idea

Clear methods + clear roles → more predictable work

TaylorStudy tasks and improve the work method.
FayolPlan, coordinate, and manage the organization.
WeberUse defined authority and consistent rules.

At the café: a standard opening checklist can prevent missed food-safety checks when the second location opens.

Useful when

Work must be reliable

Repeatable routines make training and quality control easier.

Watch for

Too many rules can slow responses to customer needs or make staff feel unheard.

Manager’s move: keep the checklist, but invite staff to improve it.
Infographic 02 / Behavioural

Pay attention to the people doing the work

The behavioural approach treats motivation, relationships, and communication as part of performance.

The central idea

Listening + support → stronger commitment

Human relationsSocial needs affect how people work.
ParticipationStaff can help shape decisions that affect them.
MotivationRecognition and growth matter alongside pay.

At the café: before changing shifts, the supervisor asks staff which hours are difficult and tests a fairer schedule.

Useful when

Teamwork affects service

Staff who can speak up may catch problems early and help customers better.

Watch for

Discussion still needs a decision, a deadline, and clear responsibilities.

Manager’s move: listen, explain the decision, then follow up.
Brochure / Modern approaches

Choose a response that fits the situation

Modern management sees connections inside and outside the organization. It does not assume one method works everywhere.

↔

Systems thinking

A change in one part of the café affects others. Faster online orders can increase kitchen pressure and change staffing needs.

Ask: what else will this change affect?
◇

Contingency thinking

The best response depends on the conditions. A busy weekend may need a tight process; testing a new menu may need more staff input.

Ask: what fits this problem now?
◎

Continuous improvement

Use results and feedback to make small changes. Compare wait times and complaints after adjusting the order process.

Ask: what did we learn?

A balanced recommendation for the second café

Use a standard food-safety routine from classical management, involve staff in scheduling from the behavioural approach, and track customer demand as the new location changes. The mix protects consistency without ignoring people or the market.
